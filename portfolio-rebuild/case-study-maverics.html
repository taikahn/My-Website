Case Study

Maverics Product Design

Over five years at Strata Identity, I helped shape the interface direction for Maverics Identity, a cloud-based security and identity orchestration platform used by enterprise customers.

Overview

Designing for a complex enterprise product

Maverics Identity required interface design that could support highly technical use cases while still feeling understandable, approachable, and consistent. My work helped move the product from exploratory wireframes into polished high-fidelity design and eventually into a live customer-facing experience.

The challenge was not just making screens look better. It was creating a more usable product structure: clearer information hierarchy, more intentional navigation, stronger page layouts, and a repeatable visual language that could scale across new product areas as the platform evolved.

“

The work was less about decorating screens and more about helping a difficult product feel organized, learnable, and trustworthy at enterprise scale.

Problem

Making technical workflows easier to understand

Identity orchestration products contain dense information, multi-step setup paths, and deeply contextual controls. Without deliberate design structure, these experiences can quickly become visually noisy and hard to learn. A key challenge was finding ways to reduce cognitive load while still exposing the flexibility advanced users needed.

Approach

From rough concepts to scalable product patterns

Workflow clarity

I focused on making complex orchestration flows feel easier to understand through cleaner visual grouping, stronger hierarchy, better spacing, and more explicit layout decisions.

Interface consistency

Reusable patterns across forms, panels, tables, navigation, and status-driven components helped create a more coherent product experience over time.

Design to production

The work was done with an eye toward what could actually be built, maintained, and expanded by engineering as the live platform matured.

Outcome

A more mature and production-ready design language

This work helped establish a stronger visual and structural foundation for the product. Instead of isolated screen designs, the focus became a broader system of patterns that improved usability, reinforced brand and product quality, and supported enterprise customer expectations.

The result was a more coherent interface language, stronger internal consistency across product surfaces, and a clearer foundation for future product growth.

It also reinforced an important principle in product design: good interface work is not only about aesthetics. It is about helping complex products become easier to learn, easier to use, and easier to expand.
